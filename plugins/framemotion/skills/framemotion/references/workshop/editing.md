# Editing inside a motion film

Read this for films with voice-over, footage, music cut to length, captions or 15/30/60-second versions. Exceptions: [decision table](decisions.md). A full edit of supplied footage belongs to an editor such as FrameCut when it is installed; this file covers the editing decisions a motion film makes itself.

**The cut (Murch's order).** Emotion, story, rhythm, eye trace, the screen plane, 3D space; sacrifice from the end of the list. Every cut has a reason (new information, a reaction, an exit from frame, a beat, a time jump, hiding a defect); without a reason, stay on the shot. After a punchline or reveal, hold for a beat. Eye trace: the important element of B sits where the eye was in A; in 9:16, faces in the upper-middle band.

**One style per film.** Invisible editing (spots, interviews) or expressive (jump, smash and match cuts, speed ramps: music video, advertising). Structure: hook, setup, escalation, punchline, resolution; advertising: problem, product, proof, offer.

**Stages.** Selection, assembly, rough cut, fine cut, picture lock, then sound, colour and captions, then export.

**Voice-over and talking head.** Choose by content (transcript) and never change the meaning by joining sentences. Silence margins of about 6 frames after and 3 frames before a word at 30 fps. Keep the breath before a new thought, loud breaths at -6 to -12 dB. Room tone only in gaps. Cover a cut with B-roll, a punch-in between 100% and 115-125% on the eyes, or an open jump cut. J and L cuts in conversation and B-roll. A micro-fade on every audio cut.

**Short form.** The first frame is the strongest picture or sentence (never a logo or a fade-in); reset attention every 1-5 s; every claim has proof in the picture; an ending that returns to the start (replay). For 15/30/60-second versions make the 15-second cut first; longer ones add context and proof.

**Cutting to music.** Cut density follows energy: every beat only at the peak or drop, every two beats usually, every bar when calm; scene changes on 4- or 8-bar boundaries. At 120 BPM a beat is 0.5 s and a bar 2 s. Shorten music by whole bars at matching points with a crossfade through a beat. Back-time so the real ending of the track lands on the end card, never a fade in mid-phrase.

**Sound order.** Dialogue, processing (high-pass 80-100 Hz, noise reduction, EQ, compression, de-ess), room tone, music, effects, mix, measurement. Music under voice-over 15-20 dB lower for dense speech; about 7 dB lower is the minimum for energetic films, then listen. A stylised transition is a sound (whoosh, hit) on the beat. Loudness: web -14 LUFS and true peak -1 dBTP; EBU R128 -23; ATSC -24; streaming dialogue -27.

**Colour order.** One conversion, exposure, white balance, contrast, skin, shot matching, then the look (LUT after correction at 50-70%). No oversaturation, crushed blacks or orange-teal on everything.

**Captions.** About 42 characters per line, two lines, at most 20 characters per second in English and 17 in Polish, inside the safe zone, large and high-contrast. 9:16 safe zone: content between about y 220 and 1440; Reels top 210-250 px and bottom 280-310 px; TikTok top 130 px, bottom 480 px and right 100-150 px.

**Export and QC.** 1080×1920, H.264 yuv420p, AAC 48 kHz, about 10 Mb/s; master ProRes 422 HQ, alpha ProRes 4444. QC the exported file, not the timeline: flash frames, black or frozen frames, clicks, A/V drift, LUFS and true peak, spelling, safe zones, duration, fps, first and last frame. Fit to fill: speed = source length / gap length.

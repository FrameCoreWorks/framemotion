# Techniques library

When to use what: [decision table](decisions.md). Motion values: [motion](motion.md). Look: [look](look.md).

**Against flatness.** Several depth planes (foreground, object, middle, background, haze) and a shared finish (grain, a subtle edge glow). A photo or illustration projected onto a simple 3D form (camera mapping; in Blender match the camera, for example with fSpy), the camera close to the projection axis because further away the stretching shows. Rim light at the edge brings out texture.

**Against a cramped frame.** Strong jumps of scale: a macro shot filling the frame alternating with a wide shot where the subject is small. Never several shots in a row at the same medium scale.

**Openings (the first frame is already in action).** A metamorphosis from frame 0 without a hold; a point in a void that unfolds into a direction; an ordinary thing doing something impossible; a flat "poster" that turns out to be 3D when it rotates; a dot with anticipation that becomes a character; sound first and one line drawing the frame; a macro riddle without the wide shot.

**Transitions out of the picture.** Part of a letter stretches into an element of the next scene; matching shape and speed across a cut; a tunnel of concentric shapes with exponential scale (GSAP ExpoScaleEase), sparingly; a metamorphosis between media (2D to 3D to cut paper); an ink or brush blot as a mask with a ragged noisy edge; a shutter: two frames of black and a focus pull. Through water: caustics and refraction flood the frame and reveal the next scene.

**Signature moves.** A delay wave from the centre of a grid (stagger by distance); frame echo, slit-scan; a whip pan with directional blur as the cut; a freeze frame as a gag (freeze at the peak of action plus a caption); kaleidoscopic symmetry; line "boil" on twos or threes for a hand-made feel; slowed footage inside a silhouette (double exposure).

**Type inside the picture.** A slight blur and an alpha threshold so letters melt into the image; joining touching letters (boolean); glow only from a source on a dark background; changing a variable font's axis instead of scale; a flash as an accent (at most three per second). More: [typography](typography.md).

**Rhythm like an accordion.** A calm start, a fast climax, a breath at the end; clean holds alternating with short bursts. Sound is designed together with the animation, not at the end.

**Colour.** A colour arc through the film (for example monochrome to full colour); colour transitions computed in OKLCH (no greying in the middle).

**Ending.** The last frame rhymes with the first; design frame 0 and the end card side by side before the middle of the film.

**Variety.** Each film takes a different outside style as its base; deliberately use a technique not done before; constrain the world instead of reaching for clichés; start from a sketch of the idea, not from the tool.

## Method

Start at the requested stage and reuse supplied decisions, approvals and the existing project. The full method is the [code-based motion workflow](references/code-based-motion-graphics.md); this entry summarizes its order.

1. **Stage and inputs.** Identify whether the request is a concept, storyboard, build, repair or review. Confirm it is time-based motion, not a static raster graphic. Collect what exists: brief, exact copy, assets, brand tokens, runtime and delivery needs. Ask only the next consequential missing question.
2. **Concept and storyboard.** When the user asks for examples, templates or inspiration, or has no idea yet, first show up to three matching entries from FrameMotion's own library: the [brief index](assets/motion-prompt-library/brief-index.json) type, the [gallery shortlist](assets/motion-prompt-library/opus-gallery-shortlist.md) (mechanism, creator, film link) and the [reel starters](assets/reel-starters/README.md); search the web only for what they lack; a Studio original or starter can become a direction as it is, a gallery film is linked and adapted (ready templates (Creative Studio: `skills/pipeline-core/references/ideation-method.md`)). For an open brief, find the idea before the look (ideation method (Creative Studio: `skills/pipeline-core/references/ideation-method.md`)): tell the user in one line which obvious motif, palette and move you set aside, and propose transformations invented for this brief (what becomes what), at most one using the category's defaults as the labelled classic. For an advert or promo, first decide the argument in [commercial motion](references/commercial-motion.md): audience, action, verified offer, benefit, friction and evidence, a few different concepts weighed internally, a hook with a payoff and a CTA that closes the shown action, recorded in the contract's `strategy`. Then test stopping power: what fills the screen in the first second, the biggest change in the film and where it lands; a clever idea that stays small on screen loses to one that strikes, and an ad must also give a reason to choose this business (a result, a proof, a person), not only list services. A reel for a local service, product or before-and-after needs pictures: the client's photos or footage, or [generated assets](references/generated-motion-assets.md) planned from the boards; without them, say before building that a type-only reel is a weaker fallback and ask where the pictures come from. Define one communicative mechanism, the scenes on one master frame timeline with readable holds, and exact copy and asset locks in one [motion contract JSON](references/motion-contract-json.md) file, using the [storyboard contract](templates/motion-storyboard-contract.md) as the field checklist. When several aspect ratios are needed, declare them as [formats](assets/motion-scenes/README.md#formats) of the same contract instead of separate projects. Before presenting it, even for a storyboard-only request, open the [gallery shortlist](assets/motion-prompt-library/opus-gallery-shortlist.md) (one small file, never the full gallery shard), take its section for the brief type and name in one line the mechanism you take and its creator with the film link; it is a read, not a build, and is not postponed to the build. When the user supplied the concept, its mechanism stays theirs: a gallery match, a stronger hook or an argument they did not ask for is only a labelled optional suggestion, and a requested length too short for readable holds is first answered with the shortest length that fits (partner rule (Creative Studio: `skills/workflow-orchestrator/references/studio-contract.md`)). Present it to the user as a storyboard table, not raw JSON; where the host composes native elements it may be a scene timeline of the same revision (presentation and interaction (Creative Studio: `skills/pipeline-core/references/presentation-and-interaction.md`)), a planning view that never exports or replaces the render. Keep Confirmed, Proposed and Unknown explicit. Present the identified revision for approval before the first build; existing approval of the same revision is enough.
3. **Motion design.** Plan and judge as a professional After Effects designer would, following [After Effects craft](references/after-effects-craft.md): one lead action and one message per beat, a written motion language (named curves, a frame-based duration scale, one stagger, an overshoot policy), blocking before curves, effects only at polish, and the After Effects term the user uses mapped to its code form. Before expanding a substantial film, judge the opening, the densest and the ending frames with exact copy and real assets at full size and phone scale. Apply [motion craft](references/motion-craft.md) for easing, durations, staggers, holds, rhythm and transitions, and [motion quality direction](references/motion-quality-direction.md) for references, style frames and a focused motion proof. Record the chosen values in the contract's motion system. Before building, run the [scene logic check](references/dense-kinetic-reels.md#scene-logic-check-run-it-on-the-storyboard-and-again-on-the-frames) on every board: each object does its real job on its material, seen from the side that shows the result, and the next board grows out of that result; colours, type and objects come from the brand, never from a reference. A dense brand showreel (chat hook, a carrier from the logo, kinetic offer boards, logo assembly) follows [dense kinetic reels](references/dense-kinetic-reels.md); a teaser, trailer or launch film in huge type on the beat follows [kinetic launch films](references/kinetic-launch-films.md) (beat sheet, energy targets, 4K master). When the brief leaves the look open, derive it from the chosen idea and execute it with a [motion style](assets/motion-styles/README.md) or values of its own instead of asking brand questions; the user's brand values always win. For a product photo, a trend or a word-by-word hook, use `photo-hero`, `line-chart` and word entries, and keep a logo or offer tag on screen with persistent elements ([scene kinds](assets/motion-scenes/README.md)). For a vertical ad, the [ad templates](assets/motion-variants/README.md) (offer, product, testimonial) render a chosen idea whose structure they fit, and `variants.py` makes brand, language and format versions; an idea that needs another structure gets scenes of its own. For a film of a product's interface, follow [product films](references/product-films.md) and the `device` scene kind with the user's screenshots. With music or voice-over, use the [sync tool](assets/motion-sync/README.md) for the beat grid and captions, and for a song's words [lyric timing](assets/motion-sync/README.md#lyric-timing). The [motion compendium](references/motion-compendium.md) maps all motion knowledge, ad creative, tools and licences. When the film needs realistic objects, products, hands, close-ups or photographic light and the user supplied none, plan [generated cut-out assets](references/generated-motion-assets.md) from the storyboard, one object per image with its transparency route for the named generator, and offer ready prompts or, with an approved connected generator, make them; never draw realistic objects or hands in code, and ask for light effects on black with no backing plate. When the user points to a motion effect on a website as the reference, measure it with the [web motion probe](assets/web-motion-probe/README.md) where a shell and a browser exist (libraries, each tween or CSS animation with its duration, delay, ease and properties, frames over time) and take values, not code: rebuild the technique in Studio's runtime and never copy the site's code, assets or text. A technique the brief names ("morph match cut") is looked up in the [technique atlas](assets/technique-atlas/README.md) after the HyperFrames registry and then written from the brief, never copied from its card. Once the idea is chosen, the library's [brief index](assets/motion-prompt-library/brief-index.json) and at most three records from the [motion prompt library](assets/motion-prompt-library/README.md), including the 703-film [Opus 5.5 gallery](assets/motion-prompt-library/README.md#opus-55-gallery), can supply technique for it; they are not a source of directions: name the mechanism taken, credit the creator's post, never paste a source prompt. For a prompt to paste into another coding agent, first open `assets/motion-prompt-library/README.md` with the file tool and follow its section [writing a prompt for a coded film](assets/motion-prompt-library/README.md#writing-a-prompt-for-a-coded-film), then the [gallery shortlist](assets/motion-prompt-library/opus-gallery-shortlist.md) for technique; never grep the full gallery shard.
4. **Runtime.** With neither a shell nor code execution (only reading and search tools), this is the no-code route: in the first answer name the [motion player](https://framecoreworks.github.io/framecore-works-creative-studio/) (Open contract) beside the storyboard as the way to watch and export the video, and after approval write the `.motion.json` contract with the exact copy in the reply; switching on a shell may be offered as an extra option, never as the only way forward. Otherwise probe before you choose or promise a runtime: `node -v`, `ffmpeg -version`, a Chrome or Chromium path and whether the npm registry answers (`npm view hyperframes version`); then tell the user in one line which route you chose and why. In Claude Code or another shell host with Node.js, FFmpeg and Chrome or Chromium, client reels and ads are HyperFrames compositions by default: follow [client reels in Claude Code](references/claude-code-reels.md) and plan them with the [reel board system](references/reel-board-system.md) (owner decision 2026-10-12). In ChatGPT Work the sandbox can run HyperFrames too: when the check finds Node.js, FFmpeg, Chromium and the npm registry, offer to install the pinned CLI for this session and make it the route for client reels (owner test 2026-10-14; the [runtime map](references/runtime-map.md#3-default-route-per-column) gives the steps); never say the sandbox cannot install it before the check. Otherwise start from the host with the [runtime map](references/runtime-map.md): it says which tools run with no code execution, with code execution or with a shell. Preserve an explicit user choice or a working project; otherwise recommend the simplest suitable available stack using the [runtime selection](references/code-based-motion-graphics.md#runtime-selection) and the [toolkit routing table](references/motion-toolkit-routing.md#choose-by-the-required-result). React/TypeScript compositions go to [Remotion Video](remotion/REMOTION.md). When the npm registry is unreachable but Node.js, FFmpeg and a browser are there, write the composition as one HTML page and render it with [`capture-html.mjs`](assets/motion-export/README.md#hand-written-html-to-mp4-without-npm); do not promise `npx hyperframes`.
5. **Build.** Work in layers (mandatory, [layered work](references/layered-work.md)): plan and grey-block blocking, typography and content, base motion without effects, depth and polish, colour and look, sound, then detail control and QC of the exported file; one layer at a time, a frame check after each, a fix returns to its own layer and the layers above are checked again, each layer its own function or file with a switch; only a very short simple job may take one pass, with the reason in the plan. Follow the [reel workflow](references/reel-workflow.md) for every reel, ad or short film: the plan approved, then a contact sheet of key frames shown in the chat and accepted, then a real 540p preview MP4 (corrections re-render only the changed part and splice it in), then the full render; a user who asks for the full render straight away gets it, and FrameMotion's own frame check always runs. Only when authorized and capable: implement the approved contract with every visual state derived from the requested frame, shared preview and export state, loaded fonts and assets, and no wall-clock or unseeded randomness. Declare scenes with the [scene kinds](assets/motion-scenes/README.md) where they fit, so the contract alone drives the preview and the Remotion render; write custom scene code only for the rest. Start from a starter when no project exists: for a client reel in HyperFrames, a [reel starter](assets/reel-starters/README.md) whose job matches the brief; the [GSAP motion starter](assets/gsap-motion-starter/README.md) for HTML, the [Remotion kinetic type starter](remotion/assets/kinetic-type-starter/README.md) for React/TypeScript, or the dependency-free [frame starter](assets/code-motion-starter/README.md). Both runtime starters read the same `motion-score.json` contract.
6. **Review and deliver.** Where Node and a local Chrome or Chromium exist, run the [automated frame review](assets/motion-review/README.md) first and repair its errors; for a composition outside the contract (HyperFrames, GSAP, hand-written HTML) run the [visible-text audit](assets/motion-review/README.md#visible-text-audit). Check generated assets with the [asset check](assets/motion-assets/README.md) before compositing, and their edges in the rendered film at peak zoom. Run [motion activity](assets/motion-quality/README.md#motion-activity) on every rendered MP4 and rework a REVISE (undeclared stills); plan change inside each board too, not only between boards. Before delivery compare the reel with the user's reference reels or the [board system](references/reel-board-system.md#2-motion-that-keeps-going) measurements (full-frame changes, scale changes, the largest moment); a reel that passes every check can still be too quiet for a client, so say so instead of calling it finished; judge whether the idea develops with the [development review](references/motion-development-review.md) (idea, composition, rhythm, cause, signature, physical logic, ending; restraint is not a defect). Check the plan and the frames for page chrome, the strongest tell of a template film: a small label parked in a corner or along an edge, a chapter counter ("02 / 04"), a timecode or progress bar, a kicker line above a headline, the same left headline block beside a card or device in scene after scene, a headline typed letter by letter. Remove each one the idea does not need; what the ad must carry (a persistent logo, the offer, phone, address) stays, placed as part of the composition (adapted from motionmaxxing, Apache-2.0). Accept in four separate verdicts (technical, fidelity, composition, temporal) with `acceptance.py` and deliver via `--pack` (for a HyperFrames or hand-written composition, `--copy` with the locked copy and `--motion-activity` with the measurement): a valid export or a contract-only score never passes the picture or the viewing. Inspect actual output at frame 0, N-1, readable holds and both sides of each boundary, watch the full sequence when possible, and inspect the encoded file. Use the [QA record](templates/motion-qa-record.md) within the shared review budget, then deliver the editable project, exports actually produced and remaining limits.

## Technical explainers and interactive figures

For report/chart animation, real-product walkthroughs, format changes or timestamped corrections, use [data-led motion and revision-bound feedback](references/data-motion-and-feedback.md). Bind displayed quantities to checked sources and scenes, distinguish reconstructed UI from captured interaction, inspect format-specific layouts and retain revision-bound feedback. Reuse existing contracts and working notes; do not invent runtime fields, live data refresh or a Claude Motion API.

For a requested coded explanation of a mechanism, process or system, use the [bundled Anatomy toolkit and method](../../integrations/anatomy/README.md) within the selected runtime. An interactive HTML/SVG deliverable stays interactive; it does not require an unsolicited MP4. Static technical graphics stay with Graphic Design. This profile does not select an engine, require isometric styling or authorize installation.

- **Map the explanation before the parts.** Identify the real inputs, outputs, units, thresholds and relationships, separating verified facts from assumptions. When a physical analogy helps, state which part/action represents each relationship and where the analogy stops. Use the detail needed for the audience; do not force a machine, a minimum part count or ornamental density onto a simple explanation. Retain useful labels and the user's visual direction.
- **Use one model.** Geometry state, moving parts, numerical readouts and captions must describe the same state at the same instant. Derive them from one small testable model; check boundary values, reset and input changes. Do not animate a plausible-looking counter independently of the mechanism or present a teaching simplification as an engineering simulation.
- **Respect both runtimes.** In interactive work, support the relevant pointer/touch and keyboard actions, clear focus, reduced motion and a meaningful still state; pause idle work offscreen. For video, replay recorded inputs or a prescribed sequence deterministically from frame/time and reset model state before seeking. Do not copy a wall-clock animation loop into the export path or claim a working interactive page proves video export.
- **Keep the parts connected.** Build static geometry once, use consistent camera/coordinates, and update only changing parts. Check attachment points, clearances, clipping and front/back order in resting, extreme and intermediate states. A quick or sampled geometry audit covers only its recorded shapes and states; inspect actual pixels as well. Never replace true geometry with a stand-in merely to silence a checker.
- **Use effects where they explain.** A shader must add information that simpler geometry cannot convey. Keep it in the same camera and masks as the illustration, with continuous transitions and a readable non-WebGL fallback. Inspect phone-size output, relevant controls, transitions and fallback within the existing review budget; do not impose a card frame, corner labels or a universal light/dark style on other motion work.

The complete SVG/WebGL/3D kit, React adapters, geometry audits, browser review tools and source examples are bundled in the [Anatomy engine](assets/anatomy-engine/README.md). Copy it into a new project with `studio.mjs init`, probe the selected runtime with its executable fixtures, then author from the brief. Its frame bridge feeds Studio's existing HTML video capture; it needs no separate Anatomy install. 3D remains upstream beta and requires project-specific render evidence. Gallery publication, session-log collection and host-security changes are outside the graphics integration.

## Deliver the video

- **With code execution but no shell,** render the MP4 from the contract with the bundled [Python renderer](assets/motion-render/README.md), copied byte for byte as `<id>.render.py` (exact size, FPS and frame count, H.264, no audio unless requested). An SVG logo needs `cairosvg`; ask for a PNG of the mark up front.
- **Improve before delivery.** Run the craft critique (`assets/motion-review/critique.py`) and make at least one improvement round when it finds an error or warning (fix, re-render, critique again; report the score before and after), within the shared budget of at most two repair rounds. Check the frames at every scene boundary, hold start and transition for overlapping, clipped or off-frame text.
- **Deliver** the MP4 download link and the actual editable project that rendered it: the `<id>.motion.json` and `<id>.render.py` for the Python route, or the source composition and build instructions for HyperFrames, Remotion or another engine ([delivery steps](assets/single-file-preview/README.md#how-studio-delivers-it)). Every scene declares a scene kind. An optional HTML preview is for watching only: no Export video button and no `MediaRecorder`. A complete copy of the player template is delivered only byte for byte with its contract replaced. Wherever a `.motion.json` exists (ChatGPT, Work, Codex, Claude Code), also give the [motion player](https://framecoreworks.github.io/framecore-works-creative-studio/) in the reply: the one-click link from the render summary's `player` (`link`, or `url` with Open contract when `link` is null; `player-link.mjs` builds it with Node), so the user can watch, scrub and export the video in the browser after the sandbox is gone.
- **With a shell, Node.js 22, FFmpeg and Chrome** (Codex or a local project), HyperFrames is the default for client reels and ads as in step 4 and also serves website-to-launch films, longer narrated videos, word-level captions or music-driven edits; Studio keeps intake, approvals and review, and the HeyGen catalog is used only on request ([HyperFrames engine](references/hyperframes-engine.md); which of its nine skills builds each job and what each requires: [skill map](references/hyperframes-skill-map.md)). Every engine, bundled or our own, follows the [engine contract](references/engine-contract.md): a pure function of time driven by one HyperFrames timeline. For photographic three.js shots (a product on a table, metal, glass, soft window light), use the [photoreal blocks](assets/engines/photoreal/README.md): sub-frame anti-aliasing, depth of field and motion blur, a film grade, baked window light, procedural materials (painted wall, oak floor, wood, lacquer, steel, fabric, plastic) and props (table top, window, logo plaque). The built-in engines (shader transitions, launch films in twelve styles and the kaventro templates, Hairline, photoreal, and library engines for shader backgrounds, picture effects, pattern grids, morphing words, particles and self-drawing sketches) are listed in [engines](assets/engines/README.md) and render with its one renderer.
- **Choosing among the look routes below:** when several fit, choose by the deliverable and say why in one line: a real mechanism with true geometry goes to the Anatomy method above; isometric line art driven by sound to Iso Glow; a calm isometric object whose one bright stroke follows the story (no glow) to Hairline; a painter's look or an explainer channel's grammar to art-style direction (with the Animate engine as its renderer when a shell exists); a whole hand-made film with its own check-ins to the Animate engine. ## Guardrails

- A work-area choice or this skill's name never selects a runtime, installs tools or authorizes rendering, uploads or publishing.
- HyperFrames is a coded-video path, not a paid media-provider integration.
- Do not use coded overlays to bypass the one-pass policy for raster graphics with visible text.
- Do not invent runtime APIs, package versions, platform safe zones or render results; check installed versions and current documentation when a named tool's behavior matters.
- Do not claim a render, playback or audio review that did not actually happen.
- Every visible word is complete in readable holds; audit flags such as `data-layout-ignore` never exempt visible text, and a deliberate exception needs `data-text-clip-ok` with a reason and a pixel review.
- Keep private paths, unapproved assets and hidden metadata out of deliverables.

## Handoff

Review gate: `structure_fit`. The workflow role `hyperframes-producer` maps to this skill and `execution-manifest` to the existing manifest owners. Keep the internal `HyperFrames Production Brief` handoff type for compatibility; it is not an engine selection. Hand off with:

## QA checklist

- The plan is time-based motion, not static raster generation.
- Every scene has a frame interval, focal point, readable hold and transition.
- Motion values come from the recorded motion system and serve the message.
- Text is readable at the intended viewing size for the full hold.
- No page chrome (corner labels, counters, timecodes, progress bars, kickers, repeated slide layouts) unless the idea needs it.
- Assets, fonts and exclusions are explicit; locks are preserved.
- Preview, temporal review, audio and encoded export are reported separately, with NOT VERIFIED where evidence is missing.

## FrameCut handoff

FrameCut can execute simple keyed transforms, per-format framing, freeze frames, variable speed and bounded effects itself. Reuse those native capabilities when sufficient; take ownership of the requested designed motion rather than rebuilding its global edit. Native composition stills and authored specialist stills feed the same complete board. A FrameCut speed change does not authorize stretching a previously bound motion return.

Honor FrameCut's stage: before storyboard acceptance, return the requested designed still states and motion/sound plan for its full-sequence contact sheets. Execute a video sample only when the user requested it or accepted the offer. Final composition rendering follows acceptance of the current complete FrameCut board; a broad build request or local sample acceptance does not replace that evidence. Keep still rendering separate from a mandatory video preview. Return a proposed creative change to FrameCut's board revision loop; technical implementation repairs that preserve the accepted design keep its acceptance.

For a FrameCut handoff, reuse its working agreement and request; author only the requested local composition at origin 0 with exact frame count/FPS, dimensions and alpha convention. Deliver `alpha: "straight"` as ProRes 4444 in a .mov and `opaque` as H.264 in an .mp4, never WebM (FrameCut reads no VP9 alpha); conform engine frames with [the FrameCut recipe](references/engine-contract.md#from-frames-to-a-framecut-motion-request). Return rendered media, the hashed authoring contract, original request and actual review/limitations. FrameCut binds them with `attach-motion --request`, checks stale context and owns global placement, audio/caption mapping and final composition. A changed request requires reconciliation, not silent stretching. Do not restart intake or create a second global timeline.
